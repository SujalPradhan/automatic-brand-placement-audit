$0.26–$0.31 to audit an hour of video

Start to finish, frame extraction included, Apple's on-device text reader on a rented Mac costs $0.26 to $0.31 per video hour and takes 12 to 15 minutes. The cloud APIs we tested cost $1.48 to $9.00 for the same hour. At 100,000 one-hour videos that is $26k to $31k against $148k to $900k.

Three things stand out:

  • Apple's reader is the cost winner. At 1 frame per second it needs about 25,167 Mac host-hours, $26k to $31k.
  • Video Intelligence is the most expensive option we measured. On its own it costs $7.20 to $9.00 per video hour and found 45% of boards.
  • Gemini finds the most boards, including 61% of the small ones, at $148k to $295k. That is 4.8–9.6× the Apple price. Whether the extra boards are worth it depends on the client.

Why ours is cheaper and faster

  1. We rent a computer, not a service. Cloud APIs add margin, support and scaling to every call.
  2. Apple's OCR runs on the Mac itself. No upload, no queue, no network.
  3. The job is narrow. We read text and match a short list of brands, and Apple's OCR does that well enough.
  4. It runs as a batch on a dedicated machine, with most cores busy and nobody else in line.
  5. We skip what vendors sell: valuation, reports, live delivery and human review.

The trade is accuracy: we find 69% of boards, Gemini 85%. We are batch, not live.

Assumptions: the EC2 Mac M4 host is assumed to match the speed of the machine we measured on, which we did not test. Cloud Vision, Gemini and Rekognition rates are list prices. The Gemini token counts came from one model and were priced at another model's confirmed rates, so the range covers both rates. The Rekognition rows are price only: we did not measure their accuracy. PaddleOCR (recall 73%) was too slow to cost and Tesseract (recall 18%) too inaccurate. Sampling every 2 seconds instead of every second would halve every figure, and we have not measured what it costs in recall.
